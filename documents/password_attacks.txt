PASSWORD ATTACKS AND DEFENSE

Password-related attacks attempt to guess, reuse, steal, or abuse authentication secrets.

Types:
- Brute force: tries many possible passwords against an account or service.
- Dictionary attack: tries likely words and common passwords.
- Password spraying: tries a small number of common passwords across many accounts.
- Credential stuffing: reuses usernames and passwords exposed in other breaches.
- Phishing: tricks users into disclosing credentials.
- Offline cracking: tests password guesses against stolen password hashes.

Protection:
- Use unique, long passwords or passphrases for every service.
- Use a reputable password manager.
- Prefer passkeys where supported and enable MFA.
- Block known compromised passwords.
- Apply rate limits, progressive delays, and sensible lockout protections.
- Monitor suspicious sign-in attempts and alert users.
- Store passwords with a modern salted password-hashing algorithm, not plaintext or reversible encryption.
- Never log passwords or expose them in error messages.
- Secure password-reset and account-recovery processes.

If an account may be compromised:
- Change the password from a trusted device.
- Revoke active sessions and unknown app tokens.
- Enable MFA and review recovery methods.
- Check for unauthorized forwarding rules, linked devices, or profile changes.
- Change reused passwords on other services.
