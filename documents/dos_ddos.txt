DENIAL-OF-SERVICE (DoS) AND DISTRIBUTED DENIAL-OF-SERVICE (DDoS)

A denial-of-service attack attempts to make a service unavailable by exhausting bandwidth, connection capacity, application resources, or other system limits. A DDoS attack uses many distributed sources.

Possible signs:
- Sudden spikes in traffic or connection requests.
- Increased latency, errors, or service outages.
- Unusual traffic patterns from many sources.
- Resource exhaustion without a normal business explanation.

Defenses:
- Use DDoS protection from a qualified provider or content delivery network.
- Apply rate limits, request-size limits, timeouts, and connection limits.
- Use caching and resilient architecture where appropriate.
- Monitor traffic and maintain an incident escalation plan.
- Coordinate with hosting providers and network operators during an attack.
- Avoid relying on a single server or network path for critical services.
- Test capacity and resilience only with authorization.

A legitimate traffic surge can resemble an attack. Investigate logs and traffic patterns before concluding that an outage is malicious.
