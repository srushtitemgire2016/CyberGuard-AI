SOCIAL ENGINEERING

Social engineering manipulates people into revealing information, transferring money, granting access, or bypassing security controls.

Common techniques:
- Pretexting: inventing a believable scenario or identity.
- Baiting: enticing someone with a tempting offer or item.
- Quid pro quo: promising a benefit in exchange for access or information.
- Tailgating: following an authorized person into a restricted area.
- Impersonation: pretending to be a colleague, vendor, executive, or support worker.
- Business email compromise: manipulating payment or data-transfer workflows.

Warning signs:
- Urgency, secrecy, pressure, or requests to bypass approvals.
- Unexpected requests for credentials, MFA codes, gift cards, or payments.
- Changes to bank details or payment instructions.
- Requests that conflict with normal procedures.

Defenses:
- Verify identity and requests through a separate trusted channel.
- Require dual approval for high-value payments or sensitive changes.
- Never share passwords or MFA codes.
- Use visitor controls and challenge unknown visitors politely.
- Encourage reporting without blame.
- Provide regular, realistic security awareness training.
