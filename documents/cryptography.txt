CRYPTOGRAPHY: ENCRYPTION, HASHING, SIGNATURES, AND TLS

Cryptography helps protect confidentiality, integrity, authenticity, and non-repudiation.

Encryption transforms readable data into ciphertext using a key. Decryption uses the appropriate key to recover the data. Encryption is used for data at rest and in transit.

Hashing maps input data to a fixed-length digest. Secure hash functions are designed to make it impractical to recover the input or find collisions. Passwords should be stored with a dedicated password-hashing scheme such as Argon2id, scrypt, or bcrypt, with salts and appropriate parameters—not with a fast general-purpose hash alone.

Digital signatures use asymmetric cryptography to help verify authenticity and integrity.

TLS helps protect network communication and authenticate servers when certificate validation is performed correctly.

Best practices:
- Use established, maintained cryptographic libraries.
- Do not invent custom encryption algorithms.
- Protect, rotate, and restrict access to keys.
- Do not hard-code secrets in public source repositories.
- Use authenticated encryption where suitable.
- Follow current standards and expert guidance for algorithm and parameter selection.
