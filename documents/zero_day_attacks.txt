ZERO-DAY VULNERABILITIES AND EXPLOITS

A zero-day vulnerability is a software flaw for which effective remediation may not yet be available or widely deployed. A zero-day exploit is a method that takes advantage of such a flaw.

Risks:
- Attackers may exploit the flaw before a patch is available.
- Affected systems may be exposed through browsers, applications, devices, or network services.
- The impact depends on the flaw, privileges, exposure, and available mitigations.

Risk reduction:
- Minimize exposed services and unnecessary software.
- Apply vendor mitigations and patches as soon as they are available.
- Use least privilege, segmentation, endpoint protection, and application control.
- Monitor vendor advisories and trusted security bulletins.
- Maintain tested backups and incident-response plans.
- Investigate unusual activity and preserve relevant logs.
- Use virtual patching or compensating controls when recommended by the vendor.

Not every vulnerability is a zero-day, and the term does not by itself describe severity. Follow authoritative vendor guidance and assess the actual affected versions.
