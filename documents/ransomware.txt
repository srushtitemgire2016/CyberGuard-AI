RANSOMWARE: PREVENTION, DETECTION, AND RECOVERY

Ransomware is malware that commonly encrypts files or systems and demands payment. Some operators also steal data and threaten to publish it.

Common entry paths:
- Phishing attachments or links.
- Exploited unpatched vulnerabilities.
- Stolen or weak remote-access credentials.
- Compromised software or third-party access.

Warning signs:
- Files suddenly become inaccessible or have unfamiliar extensions.
- Ransom notes appear.
- Unexpected mass file changes or suspicious administrative activity.
- Security alerts or unusual data transfers.

Prevention:
- Maintain offline or immutable backups and test restoration regularly.
- Patch operating systems, applications, VPNs, and remote-access services.
- Require MFA for remote access and administrator accounts.
- Limit privileges and segment networks.
- Use endpoint detection and email filtering.
- Monitor unusual file operations and outbound data transfers.
- Train staff to recognize suspicious messages.

Response:
- Activate the incident-response plan and contact security professionals.
- Isolate affected systems from networks to limit spread.
- Avoid deleting evidence or immediately wiping systems before responders assess them.
- Protect unaffected backups and systems.
- Determine the scope, entry point, and possible data theft.
- Restore only from verified clean backups after containment and remediation.
- Follow legal, regulatory, and notification requirements.
- Paying a ransom does not guarantee recovery or deletion of stolen data.
