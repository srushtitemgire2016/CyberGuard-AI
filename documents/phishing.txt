PHISHING: DEFINITION, TYPES, WARNING SIGNS, AND RESPONSE

Phishing is an attack in which someone impersonates a trusted person or organization to trick a target into revealing information, sending money, opening a malicious file, or visiting a fraudulent website.

Types:
- Email phishing: fraudulent emails sent to many targets.
- Spear phishing: tailored messages aimed at a particular person or organization.
- Whaling: targeted phishing aimed at senior or high-value individuals.
- Smishing: phishing through SMS or text messages.
- Vishing: phishing through voice calls.
- QR phishing: malicious QR codes that direct users to fraudulent websites.
- Business email compromise: impersonation or compromise of business email to request payments or sensitive data.

Warning signs:
- Unexpected requests for passwords, OTPs, payment, or confidential files.
- Urgent or threatening language.
- Sender addresses or domains that do not match the claimed organization.
- Unexpected attachments, links, QR codes, or login prompts.
- Requests to bypass normal approval procedures.
- Offers or warnings that seem unusually attractive or alarming.

Prevention:
- Verify unexpected requests through a separate, known-good contact method.
- Navigate to official sites directly rather than using message links.
- Never disclose passwords, PINs, or OTPs in response to unsolicited requests.
- Use multi-factor authentication or passkeys.
- Keep software updated and report suspicious messages.
- Use email filtering and domain protections where available.
- Do not rely on spelling mistakes alone; sophisticated phishing can look polished.

If targeted:
- Stop interacting with the message and report it.
- If credentials were entered, change them through the official service and revoke suspicious sessions.
- If payment details were shared, contact the bank or payment provider immediately.
- If a file was opened and compromise is suspected, contact IT/security support.
