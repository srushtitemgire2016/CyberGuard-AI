AUTHENTICATION AND ACCOUNT SECURITY

Authentication verifies who or what is requesting access. Authorization determines what an authenticated identity is allowed to do.

Strong controls:
- Prefer passkeys or phishing-resistant multi-factor authentication where supported.
- Use unique passwords and a password manager.
- Rate-limit repeated failed logins and monitor suspicious patterns.
- Protect password-reset and account-recovery workflows.
- Use secure session cookies and expire or revoke sessions when needed.
- Require re-authentication for high-risk actions.
- Do not reveal whether a particular account exists unnecessarily.
- Store password hashes using dedicated password-hashing algorithms and salts.
- Review privileged accounts and remove unused accounts.
- Alert users about important account changes.

MFA reduces risk but is not a guarantee. Attackers may target recovery processes, session tokens, or users through social engineering.
